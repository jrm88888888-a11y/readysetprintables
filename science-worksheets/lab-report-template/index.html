Lab Report Templates

Free printable lab report templates as PDFs. Blank sections for aim, method, results and conclusion. No sign-up and no email.

⬇ Download all 3 as one PDF — free, no sign-up

A lab report has a fixed shape, and most students lose marks not because the science is wrong but because the write-up puts the wrong thing in the wrong place — a result buried in the method, a conclusion that never mentions the aim. These templates give each section its own headed space so the structure is decided before the writing starts. They work for a single-period class practical as well as for a longer investigation, and they leave room for a labeled diagram, which most students forget to include.

Blank lab report sheets with headed sections for the aim or question, materials and equipment, method, results including space for a table or sketch, discussion and conclusion. Some layouts run to a second page for longer investigations; others fit one practical onto a single sheet. Ruled writing space throughout. Black and white, US Letter.

Frequently asked questions

What sections should a lab report have?
At minimum: the aim or question, the materials used, the method as numbered steps, the results as recorded data, and a conclusion that answers the aim. Most teachers also expect a discussion of what could have gone wrong or been improved, which is where the marks for scientific thinking usually sit.
Should the method be written in past tense?
Conventionally yes — a report describes what you did, not what someone should do, so past tense and impersonal phrasing are standard. Some schools ask for a numbered list instead of prose, which these templates accommodate since the method section is left open rather than pre-ruled into paragraphs.
How is a lab report different from a scientific method worksheet?
The worksheet is the planning tool you fill in as you go; the lab report is the finished write-up afterwards. Students often do best with both — plan on the scientific method sheet during the practical, then write it up properly on the report template.
Can I edit the section headings?
Not in the PDF itself — these are static print-ready files rather than editable documents. If your school uses different headings, the safest approach is to have students cross out and relabel, or to use the more open layouts that leave headings minimal.
