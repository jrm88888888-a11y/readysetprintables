Blank clock faces are the most reusable sheet in any time unit, because drawing the hands is a harder task than reading them and one page covers any question you care to set. Give a time, have the student draw it; give a time and ask for twenty minutes later; use one face for the start of the school day and another for the end. Frequently asked questions

How long should the hour hand be compared to the minute hand?
Noticeably shorter — about two thirds of the minute hand is the usual convention. Children often draw them the same length, which makes their own work unreadable, so it is worth insisting on the difference from the start and having them label the hands on the first few.
Where does the hour hand go for a time like 4:30?
Halfway between the 4 and the 5, not on the 4. This is the detail most often drawn wrong, and it is worth marking as an error rather than letting it pass, because the same misunderstanding is what causes misreading later.
What else can blank clock faces be used for?
Elapsed time problems with two faces side by side, timetabling a school day, matching analog to digital, and simply as a fraction model — the face divides neatly into halves, quarters and twelfths. The twelve-per-page layout also works as a quick assessment: call out twelve times and have the whole sheet filled in.
Can these be laminated and reused?
Yes, and it is worth doing for the versions with fewer, larger faces. In a plastic sleeve or laminated, a single sheet works with a dry-wipe pen for a whole term, which is handy for a daily what time is it now routine.
