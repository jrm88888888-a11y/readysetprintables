Cause and effect looks simple until you meet a real example, where one event has four causes, or one cause sets off a chain that runs for three steps before anything visible happens. That is why these come in more than one shape: a straightforward one-cause-one-effect layout, a many-to-one version for events with several contributing causes, and a chain layout where each effect becomes the next cause. Choosing the right shape for the situation is most of the analysis.

Three organizer layouts — a paired cause and effect box, a multi-cause version with several inputs feeding one outcome box, and a chain of linked boxes for sequences where effects become causes. Arrows are printed to make the direction of influence explicit. All boxes ruled for writing. Black and white, one organizer per US Letter page.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell a cause from a correlation?
Ask whether removing the supposed cause would have prevented the effect. If the answer is no, or if both things might be results of something else entirely, you have a correlation. It is a distinction worth making even with young students, using everyday examples before applying it to a text.
Which layout should I use?
Use the paired boxes for a single clear link, the multi-cause version when several factors combine — the usual case in history and science — and the chain when one thing sets off another in sequence. If students always reach for the paired version, they are probably simplifying events that had more than one cause.
What signal words show cause and effect in a text?
Because, since, therefore, as a result, consequently, so, led to, due to. Having students underline these before filling in the organizer is a fast way to find the relationships, though the most important causal links in a text are often the ones with no signal word at all.
Does this work for science as well as reading?
Yes, and it pairs naturally with experiment work — the independent variable is the cause, the dependent variable the effect. Using the same organizer in both subjects helps students see it as a way of thinking rather than a reading exercise.
