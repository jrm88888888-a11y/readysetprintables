Telling Time to the Hour Worksheets

Free printable telling time to the hour worksheets as PDFs. O'clock practice on analog clock faces. No sign-up and no email.

⬇ Download all 10 as one PDF — free, no sign-up

Telling time to the hour is the first step, and it is mostly about learning that the two hands are not equal partners. At o'clock the long hand is always straight up and the short hand does all the work, which makes it the cleanest possible introduction — one thing to read, twelve possible answers. These sheets keep the minute hand at twelve throughout so the habit that forms is reading the hour hand, rather than guessing from whichever hand the eye lands on first.

Clock faces set to whole hours with space to write the time in digits beneath, plus reverse questions where a digital time is given and the hands have to be drawn. Clock faces are printed large with clearly numbered positions and hands of visibly different lengths. Some sheets add a simple matching exercise. One worksheet per US Letter page, black and white.

Frequently asked questions

How do you explain the two hands to a child?
The short, fat hand points at the hour and the long, thin hand counts the minutes. At o'clock the long hand always points straight up at the twelve, so the only thing left to read is where the short hand is pointing. Naming the hands by length rather than by big and small avoids confusion once children compare clocks of different sizes.
What age is telling time to the hour for?
As an editorial guide, roughly ages 5 to 7. It usually comes before half hours and well before minute-by-minute reading, and children often manage it on a real clock at home before they manage it on a worksheet.
Should children learn analog or digital first?
Analog is what these sheets practice, and most curricula start there because it makes the passing of time visible — you can see how far round the hand has gone. Digital reading tends to follow quickly and needs far less teaching.
Why does my child read 3 o'clock as 12 o'clock?
Because they are reading the long hand, which points at the twelve. It is the single most common early mistake and it is worth naming explicitly: the long hand at the top does not mean twelve o'clock, it means o'clock. These sheets keep the minute hand fixed so that distinction gets plenty of practice.
