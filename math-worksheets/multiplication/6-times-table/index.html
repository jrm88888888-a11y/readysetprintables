6 Times Table Worksheets

Free printable 6 times table worksheets as PDFs. Ordered, mixed and missing-number sheets for 6 x 1 to 6 x 12. No sign-up.

⬇ Download all 10 as one PDF — free, no sign-up

The six times table sits in the awkward middle of the grid, past the easy patterns and before the nines with their finger trick. It has one quiet pattern worth knowing: six times any even number ends in that same even digit — 6 x 4 is 24, 6 x 6 is 36, 6 x 8 is 48. Beyond that the reliable route is doubling the three times table, which is why these sheets are worth doing after the threes rather than before, and why several of them pair the two facts side by side.

Sheets covering 6 x 1 to 6 x 12 in order, then in mixed order, with missing-number rows where either the product or a factor is blank. Some pages set the matching three times fact beside each six fact to make the doubling relationship visible. Division facts appear on the later sheets. One worksheet per US Letter page, black and white.

Frequently asked questions

What is the trick for the 6 times table?
Two are worth knowing. Six times an even number ends in that same even digit, so 6 x 6 must end in 6 and 6 x 8 must end in 8. And every six fact is double the matching three fact, so if you know 3 x 7 is 21, then 6 x 7 is 42.
Why is the 6 times table considered difficult?
Because it has no single memorable rule the way the nines and tens do, and it sits among facts children often have not fully secured. The individual facts are not harder; there is just less scaffolding, so it usually needs more repetition than the tables around it.
Which 6 facts do children get wrong most often?
6 x 7 and 6 x 8 are commonly reported as sticking points, partly because they overlap with the difficult end of the seven and eight tables. It is worth drilling those two specifically rather than repeating the whole table, since the rest is usually secure.
How does the 6 times table relate to time and dozens?
Six is half a dozen and six times ten is an hour in minutes, so this table quietly underpins a lot of everyday counting. Pointing out that 6 x 5 is thirty minutes and 6 x 10 is sixty gives it a use outside the worksheet.
