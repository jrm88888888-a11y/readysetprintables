Spider Map Graphic Organizers

Free printable spider map templates as PDFs. Blank webs with a center circle and labeled legs. No sign-up and no email needed.

A spider map is the organizer to reach for when one topic has several distinct branches and each branch has its own details. The topic sits in the middle, the main ideas run out as legs, and the smaller lines off each leg hold the supporting detail. It is the shape students naturally draw when brainstorming, which is exactly why a printed version helps: the freehand version always runs out of room on the busiest leg and crams three ideas into a corner.

Blank spider maps with a central circle for the topic and radiating legs, each with short branch lines for details. Versions differ in leg count — four for a tightly focused topic, six or eight for a broad brainstorm — so you can match the template to the task rather than leaving legs empty. Lines are ruled for writing. Black and white, one map per US Letter page.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use a spider map instead of a mind map?
A spider map has a fixed structure — one center, defined legs, short detail branches — which makes it better for organizing material you already have. A mind map grows freely in any direction and suits open brainstorming. If students need to sort known information into categories, the spider map's constraints help.
What goes on the legs versus the branches?
Legs hold the main categories or subtopics; the shorter branches off each leg hold specific facts, examples or details. Keeping that hierarchy consistent is most of the value, since it forces the distinction between a main idea and a supporting one.
What subjects does this suit?
It works anywhere one subject splits into parallel aspects — a country's geography, climate and economy; a character's actions, words and relationships; an animal's habitat, diet and behavior. It is less useful for anything sequential, where a sequence chart or story map fits better.
Is there a filled-in example?
These are blank templates only, so the sheets arrive empty and ready to use. If you want a worked example for a class, filling one in yourself under a document camera is usually more effective than a printed one, because students see the decisions being made.
