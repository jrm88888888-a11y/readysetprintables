Scientific Method Worksheets

Free printable scientific method worksheets as PDFs. Blank step-by-step sheets for any experiment, no sign-up and no email required.

The scientific method is easy to recite and hard to actually follow, which is why a blank sheet with the steps already laid out does more good than a poster of them. These worksheets walk a student from the question through the hypothesis, the procedure, the results and the conclusion, giving each step its own labeled space so nothing gets skipped or collapsed into a paragraph of narrative. They are deliberately subject-neutral: the same sheet works for a plant growth experiment, a paper airplane trial or a kitchen chemistry demonstration.

Blank scientific method sheets with each stage in its own bordered section — question, hypothesis, materials, procedure, observations, results, conclusion — sized so a student has real room to write rather than a single ruled line. Layouts vary in how much scaffolding they provide, from short prompts for younger students to open sections for older ones. Black and white, one sheet per US Letter page.

Frequently asked questions

What are the steps of the scientific method on these sheets?
Ask a question, do background thinking, form a hypothesis, plan and carry out a test, record observations, analyze the results, and state a conclusion that refers back to the hypothesis. Different textbooks number these differently — some merge observation and analysis — so the sheets keep the sections clearly labeled rather than numbered rigidly.
What age or grade are these for?
As an editorial suggestion, the more scaffolded layouts suit roughly ages 8 to 11 and the open ones suit older students and science fair work. The steps themselves do not change with age; what changes is how much prompting a student needs in each box.
Can I use one sheet for a whole science fair project?
It works well as the planning document, and many students then expand each section onto a board or a report. Because the sheet forces you to write the hypothesis before the procedure, it also catches the most common science fair problem — designing the experiment first and inventing the hypothesis to match.
Are these fillable on a computer?
No. They are print-ready PDFs designed to be filled in by hand, which is usually what a lab or classroom situation calls for. Print as many copies as you need for your own classroom or home.
