Six by six is the smallest grid the rules allow to be interesting, and the easy set keeps enough digits pre-filled that the first few moves are always visible. Each row has to end up with three 0s and three 1s, so the moment a row shows three of one digit, the remaining cells are settled without any further thinking. Frequently asked questions

How many 0s and 1s go in each row of a 6x6?
Exactly three of each. As soon as you have placed three 1s in a row, every remaining cell in that row must be a 0, and the same in reverse. Counting as you go is the single most useful habit on this size of grid.
My grid has two identical rows. What went wrong?
Somewhere earlier you filled a cell by counting rather than by a forced deduction. The no-duplicate-rows rule is usually the last one to bite, so when it does, the error is often several moves back. Working in pencil and marking which cells were forced makes it easy to unwind.
Is this suitable for a child?
As an editorial suggestion, roughly ages 8 to 10 and up for the easy 6x6 set. There is no arithmetic at all — the whole puzzle is pattern spotting and counting to three — so the barrier is patience rather than math ability.
Can I write X and O instead of 0 and 1?
Yes, and some solvers find symbols easier to scan than digits because 0 and 1 look similar at a glance. The logic is identical whichever pair of marks you use.
