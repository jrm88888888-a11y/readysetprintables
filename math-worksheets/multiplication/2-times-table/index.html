2 Times Table Worksheets

Free printable 2 times table worksheets as PDFs. Doubling practice, mixed order and missing number sheets. No sign-up, no email.

The two times table is really doubling with a different name, and children who already know that four and four make eight often do not realize they have half the table already. These sheets lean on that: they start in order so the pattern of even numbers is obvious, then mix the order so the facts have to be recalled rather than counted along. It is the first table most children meet and the one that makes every later table easier, because doubling turns up again in the four, six and eight tables.

Sheets that work through 2 x 1 up to 2 x 12 in order, then in mixed order, plus missing-number rows where the answer is given and a factor is blank. Later sheets turn the facts around as division questions. Layouts stay uncluttered with generous spacing between rows. One worksheet per US Letter page, black and white.

Frequently asked questions

What is the pattern in the 2 times table?
Every answer is an even number, and each one is two more than the last: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and so on. Every answer also ends in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8, which gives a quick way to spot a wrong answer without recalculating it.
Is the 2 times table the same as doubling?
Yes. 2 x 7 and double 7 are the same question, and saying so out loud helps children who find doubles easy but tables intimidating. It also works in reverse: half of 16 is the same as asking what times 2 makes 16.
What age do children usually learn it?
As an editorial guide, most children meet the two times table around ages 6 to 7, often alongside the fives and tens. Skip counting in twos usually comes first, and the written facts follow once the count is secure.
What should come after the 2 times table?
The tens and fives are the usual next step because their patterns are just as visible. After that, the four times table is a natural follow-on, since it is doubling applied twice to the same number.
