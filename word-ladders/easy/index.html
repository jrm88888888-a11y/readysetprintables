These are the first word ladders a child can do alone. Every word on every sheet is a consonant-vowel-consonant word — cat, pin, hug — so a reader who knows short vowel sounds can sound out each rung rather than guess. Each ladder is four steps, and the start and end words are shown in letter boxes so the child can see exactly which letter has to change.

10 sheets, four ladders each, on US Letter. The answer page behind each sheet highlights the letter that changed at every step. The word list is restricted to decodable CVC words: no tricky words, no vowel digraphs, no r-controlled vowels.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CVC word ladder?
A ladder in which every word is consonant-vowel-consonant (CAT, COT, DOT, DOG). Because each word is decodable, the puzzle practises short vowel sounds and letter substitution at the same time, which is why these suit kindergarten and 1st grade phonics work.
How do I introduce word ladders to a 5-year-old?
Do the first ladder together, saying each word aloud. Point to the letter that changes and ask what sound it makes. After one or two shared ladders most children can finish the sheet on their own.
Is there only one answer?
No. The answer page shows one solution. Any ladder where each step changes one letter and makes a real word is also correct, and it is worth praising a child who finds a different route.
